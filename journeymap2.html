Parent Setting Up and Monitoring a Child's Independent Reading on Epic!

User Profiles (Dual Actors)

👩

Aisha (Parent)

Mother of an 8-year-old
Moderate tech comfort
Wants to encourage nightly reading
Works evenings, needs child to read independently

👦

Jayden (Child)

3rd grader, age 8
Loves dinosaurs and comics
Reluctant reader with chapter books
Prefers tablet over physical books

⚠️ This journey tracks two actors — the parent sets up and monitors, while the child discovers and reads. Their paths diverge after Stage 2.

Scenario

Aisha signs up for Epic! to build Jayden's independent reading habit. She sets up his profile, curates initial book selections, then hands the tablet to Jayden to explore and read on his own. Aisha later checks the parent dashboard to see what Jayden read and for how long.

User Goals & Expectations

Aisha: Set up safely, curate age-appropriate content, track reading time and progress, build a nightly routine

Jayden: Find fun books he actually wants to read, browse independently, not feel like "homework"

🪦 Revised Assumptions

  • Free version would include enough books to sustain interest
  • Reading level filter would match school's Lexile levels
  • Child could browse without encountering subscription upsells
  • Parent dashboard would show comprehension, not just minutes

Section 3: Analysis

Opportunities & Insights

✓ What's Working
Interest-based profile setup creates immediate engagement. Jayden sees dinosaur books right away — personalization drives first session success.
Stage: Set Up, Explore
! Friction Point
Reading level system uses proprietary scale instead of school-standard Lexile or Guided Reading levels. Parent can't easily match to teacher's recommendations.
Stage: Curate
Heuristic #2: Match Real World
! Friction Point
Child encounters locked books mid-browse with no warning. The subscription upsell interrupts flow and creates frustration during an otherwise positive experience.
Stage: Explore & Read
Heuristic #3: User Control
✓ What's Working
Comic-format books engage a reluctant reader. Multiple format options (comics, illustrated, chapter books) mean something appeals to every reader type.
Stage: Explore & Read
UDL: Multiple Means of Engagement
! Friction Point
Parent dashboard shows time-on-app but reading time doesn't match tablet time. No comprehension indicators — parent can't assess actual learning.
Stage: Monitor
Heuristic #1: Visibility
💡 Opportunity
Add Lexile/Guided Reading level mapping so parents can filter by school-standard levels. Include a "teacher recommended" badge for books commonly assigned.
Stage: Curate
💡 Opportunity
Hide locked books from child's browse view during trial, or clearly mark them before the child taps. Prevent interruption of reading flow with upsell modals.
Stage: Explore & Read
💡 Opportunity
Add simple post-reading questions ("What was your favorite part?") and show results in parent dashboard alongside time. Bridge the comprehension data gap.
Stage: Monitor
? Open Questions
Does the child return to books they started, or start new ones each time? How does format preference (comic vs. chapter) correlate with reading duration?
Stage: Explore, Sustain
? Open Questions
What percentage of trial users convert to paid? What's the top reason parents cancel — price, content quality, or lack of progress data?
Stage: Sustain
✓ What's Working
Child-initiated return is the strongest signal. Jayden asked to read again unprompted — the product is building intrinsic motivation.
Stage: Sustain
$ Learning Impact
If reading habit sustains, child moves from 0 voluntary reading sessions/week to 5+. This aligns with research showing 20 min/day of independent reading improves fluency significantly.
Stage: Sustain
